One of my favorite things to grow in the summertime is Chinese eggplant. This long, tender vegetable is so productive and easy to grow, and the flowers and plant are surprisingly gorgeous.

Having a surplus of eggplant, however, meant I needed the perfect recipe. I've always been obsessed with the eggplant tofu dishes you get at Chinese restaurants, and I needed a good technique to get the texture just right: crispy and browned, perfect for braising in a savory aromatic sauce. I finally found success with this one, which I adapted from [Omnivore's Cookbook](https://omnivorescookbook.com/chinese-eggplant-with-garlic-sauce/).
## A note on preparation
**The key to delicious eggplant lies in its preparation.** I cut my long eggplants into half-inch thick slices, slightly on the diagonal to maximize surface area for browning, then lie the slices on a paper towel and sprinkle both sides with kosher salt.
After 5-10 minutes, I pat-squeeze the eggplant slices dry to get as much excess moisture out as possible. The eggplant then gets moved to a shallow bowl and tossed in a thin, even coating of cornstarch to promote crispiness before pan-frying.
## Ingredients
- [ ] 2-3 small Asian eggplants*
- [ ] 1 tablespoon cornstarch**
- [ ] 1 teaspoon salt
- [ ] Green onion, for garnish
- [ ] Sesame seeds, for garnish
### Sauce
- [ ] 1 tablespoon soy sauce
- [ ] 1/2 tablespoon Shaoxing wine
- [ ] 1 1/2 tablespoons water
- [ ] 2 teaspoons sugar
- [ ] 1 teaspoon cornstarch
### Stir-fry
- [ ] 2-3 cloves of garlic, chopped
- [ ] 1 teaspoon ginger, grated or minced
- [ ] Neutral oil

\* *You could theoretically use the globular, Western-style eggplants instead, but the Asian varieties are most ideal.*

\** *Can be substituted for potato starch.*

> [!tip] Hot tip!
 > I'm often lazy and use the frozen pre-grated cubes of ginger and garlic from Trader Joe's. 

## Instructions
### Step 1
Place the sliced eggplant on a paper towel and lightly salt both sides. Let sit for ten minutes, then thoroughly pat dry.
### Step 2
Combine all the sauce ingredients in a small bowl and whisk. 
*Tip: It may be easier to dissolve the cornstarch with water separately, before adding to the rest of the ingredients.*
### Step 3
Move eggplant to a shallow bowl and toss with cornstarch until each slice is evenly coated. 
### Step 4
Add 2 tablespoons oil into a large nonstick pan and heat over medium-high. Once hot, spread eggplant across the bottom without crowding or overlapping. Cook each side for 3-5 minutes before flipping, or until the cornstarch is nicely charred and crisp and the eggplant is softened. Transfer eggplant to a plate.
### Step 5
Add ~1/2 teaspoon of oil back into the pan, alongside the ginger and garlic. Stir until fragrant, then add the eggplant back into the pan. Pour sauce over eggplant and stir immediately until thickened. 
### Step 6
Transfer to a plate and garnish with chopped green onion and sesame seeds. Enjoy hot with rice or noodles!
